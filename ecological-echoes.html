BEHIND THE WORK

Listening as a
research practice.

Listening to ecological loss.

This work critiques the ecological consequences of rapid human development. Moving from a richly detailed forest soundscape towards pollution, industrial noise and crisis, it asks listeners to recognise what is lost when human activity overwhelms the living world.

Sound is approached as both a trace of an environment and a carrier of memory, control and meaning. The ending offers no single answer: it leaves space for loss, reflection and possible renewal.

Three connected concerns

  • What is lost when industrial noise obscures ecological detail?
  • How do familiar sounds trigger environmental memory?
  • What changes when observation becomes data, music and interpretation?

Walk, record, compose.

Moving through forests, beaches and urban environments made the recordist’s footsteps, breath and microphone placement part of the material. The composition combines field recordings, sourced sounds and data-generated music.

  1. Collect. Listen while walking; record environments and photograph litter.
  2. Translate. Use photographic location data and counts as inputs to musical patterns, using TwoTone, p5.js and Tone.js.
  3. Compose. Arrange and process recordings, MIDI and symbolic instruments in GarageBand and Adobe Premiere Pro.
  4. Reflect. Consider how selection, processing and the act of recording affect what is heard and what remains absent.

The narrative structure

The paper’s “Narrative Structure” outlines five conceptual movements. Its more detailed “Audioscape analysis” develops six timed passages. This listening page follows those six passages (0:00, 1:04, 1:45, 2:43, 4:18 and 4:51); individual sound timings follow the glossary.

The visual interpretation

Supplied photographs and field footage form a layered visual essay. Torn photographic fragments bring collected litter, recording walks, industrial pressure and disaster into the natural landscape. The tree-mounted cans and original making-process screenshots give the work a documentary thread. Reference photographs function as visual metaphors and are identified in the field archive. The visual score unfolds automatically with the composition: digital structures become more insistent as human control and ecological conflict intensify, then recede towards an unresolved ending. This treatment was created for the website; it supports the artist’s narrative and is not a claim that these visual effects formed part of the original audio work.

Visuals interpret the work; they are not environmental measurements. The waveform and audio-reactive energy come from the recording. Material entry and exit times come from the glossary.
